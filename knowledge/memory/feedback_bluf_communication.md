Malachi is adopting **BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)** as a standing principle for how he presents to people — meetings, Slack, email, standup, verbal summaries, and any deliverable. State the bottom line (answer / recommendation / ask) in the first sentence, then give supporting detail only if it changes what the listener decides or does.

**Why:** He's often the person explaining why a system behaves a certain way to directors and cross-functional partners who don't read thoroughly. BLUF ensures the one thing they need lands first, before attention drops. It's the verbal/human-facing extension of the written Terse Comms Standard.

**How to apply:**
- When I draft anything he'll relay to people (Slack msg, email, Jira comment, talking points, meeting summary), structure it BLUF: answer line → support.
- When I summarize findings back to him, lead with the bottom line so he can repeat it verbatim.
- BLUF-test any draft: could the reader stop after sentence one and still have the decision-relevant point? If not, rewrite the opener.
