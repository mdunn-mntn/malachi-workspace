When an analysis gets conceptually deep (billing grains, credit-attribution, lens
reconciliation), the user asks to "clear it up", "explain more simply", or "can I see this
visually?" — and once said outright "im not smart!" (self-deprecating; they're not — the
material is genuinely subtle). They want the idea landed, not just stated.

**Why:** the user is the one who has to explain this to stakeholders (Alyson, Mike, Kale). A
number they can't re-derive or a distinction they can't articulate is useless to them, however
rigorous. Their repeated "help me understand" is a signal the explanation hasn't landed yet, not
that they're slow.

**How to apply:** for a subtle point — (1) lead with the plain-English answer to their literal
question, (2) use one concrete everyday analogy (the coffee-shop / "who caused the visit"
analogy worked for impression-vs-membership grain), (3) offer a visual (house-standard PNG
charts, or a walkthrough) — don't wait to be asked twice. Drop the jargon ("grain",
"cohort", "denominator") or define it once in plain words. Keep the rigor in the docs; keep the
conversation simple. Do NOT be condescending about the "not smart" comment — affirm the question
is a hard one. Related: [[feedback_doc_style]] (one doc, tells a story, actionable).
