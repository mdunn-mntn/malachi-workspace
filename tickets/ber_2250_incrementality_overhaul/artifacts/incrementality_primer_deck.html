The One Idea Everything Is Built On

Y(1) = outcome if they see the ad
Y(0) = outcome if they don't

Causal effect = Y(1) − Y(0)

Rubin (1974) · The potential outcomes framework

The Fundamental Problem

You only ever observe one.

The household either saw the ad or didn't.
The other universe is permanently unobservable.

Every method in this talk is a strategy
for estimating the missing half.

Why Ads Are Especially Hard

1. Selection bias — we target people likely to convert
2. Activity bias — heavy users see more ads AND buy more
3. Confounding — holidays boost both spend and sales

All three inflate every ROAS number.

View-through ROAS looks high
even if the ad did nothing.

The targeting works — that's the problem.
It works so well that post-exposure conversion rates look high
even when the ad itself contributed zero.

Five Strategies for the Missing Half

1. Randomization (RCTs, ghost ads)
2. Geo experiments (the pragmatic RCT)
3. Quasi-experiments (DiD, synthetic control)
4. Marketing Mix Modeling (the top-down view)
5. Causal ML (handle with care)

Strategy 1

Randomization

If you flip a coin to decide who sees the ad,
the two groups are identical in every way
except exposure.

Any outcome difference must be caused by the ad.
The coin doesn't care about intent, income, or device.

Strategy 1 · Ghost Ads

The Clever Version of an RCT

Treatment Selected by targeting
Sees the ad
Control Selected by targeting
Sees nothing (or PSA)

Both groups were selected. Only one was exposed.

Removes selection bias without losing auction-level comparability.

Strategy 1 · Estimators

ITT vs TOT

Intent-to-Treat (ITT) Compare groups as assigned
Conservative · Clean · Easy to interpret
What we should report
Treatment-on-Treated (TOT) Adjust for who actually saw the ad
Closer to the stakeholder question
What leadership wants to hear

The difference matters. ITT biases toward zero when coverage is low.

Strategy 1 · CTV Challenges

Why RCTs Are Hard in CTV

1. No persistent user ID — household is the unit, co-viewing is real
2. Ghost-ad infrastructure doesn't exist in our bidder yet
3. Low conversion rates need huge samples to detect modest lifts

Lewis & Rao (2015): most ad experiments are underpowered

Strategy 2

Geo Experiments

Can't randomize households?
Randomize geographies.

Split DMAs into treatment and control.
Some get your CTV campaign. Others don't.
Compare total sales.

Strategy 2 · Why It Fits CTV

Geo Experiments + CTV = Natural Fit

✓ No user IDs needed
✓ Walled gardens don't matter (measuring total sales)
✓ Co-viewing doesn't matter (a geo is a geo)
✓ MNTN already partners with Haus for this

Haus, LiftLab, Measured, and Meta GeoLift all use this method.

Strategy 2 · Challenges

Geo Is Not Free

✗ 41% of CTV effect shows up after campaign ends — window matters
✗ DMAs aren't interchangeable (NYC ≠ Omaha) — need synthetic controls
✗ Only ~210 DMAs — power is limited for smaller advertisers

Haus BFCM 2025 report

Strategy 3

Quasi-Experiments

When you can't randomize, exploit
something that looks like randomization.

Strategy 3 · Difference-in-Differences

DiD: The Workhorse

(CA after − CA before) − (OR after − OR before)

Cancels out baseline differences AND shared trends.
What's left is the causal effect.

Big assumption: parallel trends

Without the campaign, CA and OR would have moved in sync.

Strategy 3 · Synthetic Control

CausalImpact / BSTS

Build a "synthetic California" from weighted other states.

Synthetic CA = 30% AZ + 25% NV + 20% TX + 25% CO

Chosen to match real CA historically.
Post-treatment gap = causal effect.

We already use this at MNTN (TI-748).

Strategy 5

Causal ML

Mathematically beautiful.
Operationally dangerous.

Propensity matching, doubly-robust estimators, uplift models, causal forests.
All rely on no unmeasured confounders.
In ads, you always have unmeasured confounders.

Attribution vs Incrementality

Attribution "Among paths that converted,
how do we divide credit?"

Journey question
Incrementality "Would they have converted
without the ad?"

Causal question

Both useful. Only one is causal. Don't confuse them.

The Attribution Trap

Your CTV ROAS might be:

Last-click$3.50
Multi-touch$2.80
View-through$2.40
Shapley$1.90

All from the same data.
None tell you if CTV caused any sales.

How Power Works

Power = probability of detecting an effect that actually exists.

1. Effect size — bigger effects are easier to detect
2. Sample size — more observations = more power
3. Variance — noisier outcomes = less power

If your MDE is 15% but the true lift is 5%,
don't run the experiment.

The Lewis-Rao Reality Check

Most ad experiments are underpowered.

A null result often means "not enough data" — not "no effect."

CTV specifics: low conversion rates + modest lifts
+ household-level measurement = structurally hard.

Lewis & Rao (2015), Quarterly Journal of Economics
