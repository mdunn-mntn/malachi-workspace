Two stories. Both about the same advertisers. One is wrong.

Clickpass said MNTN drove 5–10× more visits at high intent.
ITT (intent-to-treat) said MNTN drove zero incremental visits.

Both can't be right. Today we measured the truth — using the same hash that production uses to assign holdouts.

What we measured — ghost-bidding ATT

The problem with ITT: only 14–16% of "treated" IPs were actually served an impression. The other 84% are non-comparable — they diluted any real lift toward zero.

The fix: compare IPs that were served against IPs that would have been served if not for the holdout flag.

"Would have been served" = appeared in augmentor_log during the window. The bidder considered them eligible to bid on; the only reason they weren't served is the 10% holdout assignment.

At peak intent — clickpass shows only a third of the real lift

IVW (left) is the default pool. It collapses to ≈1.0× — which would say there's no wedge. The other three methods all show clickpass at ~30% of guid. Slide 8 explains why IVW lies here.

Why IVW hides the peak under-credit

Inverse-variance weighting gives each cell weight 1/var = n / [p(1−p)]. A cell with very small ATT and very small visit rate has vanishing variance — and gets a huge IVW weight.

At peak intent, 8 of 19 advertisers (Casper, Re-Bath, NET-A-PORTER, Overjet, Swatch, Longines, Outback, UD-Daniels) have ~0pp ATT in both arms → near-zero variance → outsized weight. They drag the IVW pool to 1.00×.

The other 11 advertisers all show wedge between 0.10× and 0.50× (clickpass under-credits guid). Median: 0.30×. Sample-weighted: 0.34×. The pattern is real — the pool method just hides it.

Methodology rule: IVW is the right tool when cells are well-powered with similar variance. It collapses to noise-floor cells when many cells have tiny ATT and tiny variance. For peak/mid reporting, prefer sample-size-weighted or median pooling.

Same MD5 holdout hash production uses. Same window for both arms (3-day post for cross-day visits). Single batched augmentor scan amortizes across all 30 advertisers (~$90 cost / 87 min).

What I'd want a methodologist to push on

  1. Single window. One 7-day analysis (2026-04-20 → 04-26). No cross-window validation yet — the pattern could shift in a different week. Augmentor 10-day TTL bounds backward replication; forward replication is straightforward.
  2. Loose biddable-holdout filter. Currently "any augmentor row" qualifies as biddable. Tighter options (advertiser-targeting match, intent-gate match, real-bid-for-this-advertiser) would tighten the counterfactual but shrink the holdout pool. Treated arm uses identical filter — comparison is internally consistent, but the level of the ATT may shift under tightening.
  3. MAX-tier subject construction. Each (advertiser, IP) gets its strongest-observed tier across the week. Tier-collapse advertisers were filtered out, but the multi-tier IPs that remain still get assigned to their MAX tier — peak/mid pools may be slightly biased upward by IPs that occasionally hit high. Cross-validation on per-day subjects deferred.
  4. Visits, not conversions. Visits are 10–20× more frequent than conversions. The wedge pattern at peak may not replicate on the rarer outcome. Conversions analysis is the immediate next step.

What's next

  • Conversions outcome. Same pipeline, swap ui_conversions for guid_log. Conversions are 10-20× rarer than visits → need ~30-day window for power. Augmentor 10-day TTL is the binding constraint — bidder-level ghost bidding (next bullet) would solve it.
  • Bidder-level ghost bidding. Production solution that escapes the augmentor TTL. Pending Alex Bloore decision; Zach + Jordan on the bidder team. This unlocks longer windows and cross-window validation.
  • iROAS. Per-advertiser (incremental conversions × AOV) ÷ MNTN spend. The number leadership actually wants. Depends on the conversions outcome above + advertiser AOV from ui_conversions.order_amt.
