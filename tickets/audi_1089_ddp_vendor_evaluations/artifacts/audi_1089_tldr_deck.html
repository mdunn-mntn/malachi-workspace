HOW WE VALUE A DATA VENDOR

A vendor is worth the margin its data lets us keep,
minus what the data costs us.

The logic

  1. A vendor makes us money one way: its data lets us serve impressions we bill to clients.
  2. Only incremental impressions count. If we would have served the impression anyway — from our own free sources or another vendor already on the roster — the vendor gets no credit for it.
  3. Revenue = volume × price. We keep only the margin M. From that, subtract the data cost.

The formula

Net Kept Marginᵥ = (Iᵥ ÷ 1,000) × CPM × M − Dᵥ

Iᵥ   — unique (IP × Domain × Date) impressions served only because of vendor v
       (net of free sources; leave-one-out against the rest of the paid roster)
CPM — client-facing price per 1,000 served impressions
M    — gross margin as a % of revenue
Dᵥ   — data cost paid to vendor v over the same window

Positive → the vendor pays for itself. Negative → we pay more for the data than the margin it creates.
