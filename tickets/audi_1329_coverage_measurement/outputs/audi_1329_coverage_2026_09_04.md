# AUDI-1329: Coverage measurement — what the debugger catches and what the optimizer scans

**The debugger names a root cause for 88% of the failures Airflow reports, and 0% of the failures Airflow does not report.** (215 of 245 classified in the local corpus, `[measured]`; 90.5% of terminal failures in production, `[measured]`. Against the silent class — 33 lost output partitions in 30 days behind green runs — recall is zero by construction.)

**The optimizer scans a mean of 215 of the 763 Spark task instances the fleet runs a day: 28%.** (Best sweep 40% after de-duplication, worst 20%, on 6 of the 14 published sweeps. `[measured]`.)

**Neither number is capped by retention.** Retention removes nothing either tool could otherwise read. The optimizer's gap is our own code plus engine opacity; the debugger's gap is a detector backlog, not expired evidence. The one place retention genuinely binds is the debugger's *retrospective* Dataproc deep-dive, which is not how the tool runs in production.

## 1. What "coverage" means on each axis, and which denominator

**The raw ratio overstates, and by a known amount.** Every sweep's acquisition window, measured from the app-id timestamps of the logs it scanned, spans 28.0–40.0 hours (median 31.1 h), never 24, while the sweep repeats every 24 h `[measured]`. A per-sweep numerator over a per-day denominator therefore double-counts. De-duplicating against the prior sweep gives 305 genuinely new runs on the best sweep, and that is the figure to quote.

**The report date is a day behind the window it covers.** Airflow's `ds` is the interval start, so `optimizer_backlog_2026-09-03.md` is the sweep that ran 2026-09-04T09:19:21Z. 256 of its 344 sources carry a 2026-09-04 app id and only 84 carry 2026-09-03 `[measured]`. The "2026-09-04 sweep" named in the framing is this file.

**Six of fourteen sweeps published a confident backlog on half the input, with no error surfaced.** On 2026-08-27 through 09-01 the source line reads verbatim:

```
Source: gs://mntn-data-archive-prod/spark-events (newest 6 of 200, 194 failed) + 150 PHS
Fleet optimization: 154 jobs scanned, 75 findings, 47 high-impact.
```

The **archive** half collapsed — 6 objects of 200 downloaded, 194 failures — and 6 + 150 PHS = 156 objects yielded 154 scanned jobs, against 344/345 on a healthy day `[measured, verified by reading all 14 headers directly]`. Findings collapsed with it, from ~300–384 down to 61–75. Nothing in the digest flags a degraded run. The likely cause is the defect `fetch.py`'s own docstring records: `gsutil` quietly landing ~2 of ~200 objects inside Astro task pods since ~2026-08-28 `[estimated — the docstring and the 08-27 plateau start agree, but I did not reproduce the pod-level failure]`. The same silent-zero path exists symmetrically on the PHS side: `phs.list_batches` returns `[]` on any non-zero `gcloud` exit or 300 s timeout (`phs.py:35-38`, no raise), and `dags/spark_optimizer_daily.py` wraps the call in `except Exception → phs_n = 0` with only a `logger.warning`; the surviving half's `n > 0` satisfies the "refusing to publish an empty sweep" guard.

**The sweep window is a fixed clock window, not a sample.** `phs.list_batches(limit=500)` at `phs.py:31` covers **7.5–7.8 hours** measured at the real 09:00 UTC run time (09-02: 01:07:38–08:58:02; 09-03: 01:23:46–08:52:17; 09-04: 01:16:27–08:50:29) `[measured]`. An earlier probe run at ~21:35 UTC reported 11.3 h; that is the wrong hour and should be discarded. The fleet is front-loaded — 59–90 batches/hr from 00:00–08:00 UTC against a flat 44–48/hr for the other 16 hours `[measured]` — so the sweep reads the busiest 7.5 hours and **16.5 of every 24 hours is unreachable on the PHS path, every day, permanently.** Any DAG whose Spark runs outside ~01:20–08:55 UTC is invisible on that path always, not sampled.

**Whose tool is it: neither.** Both tools key on the job's own reported state — the debugger on a failure, the optimizer on a run that happened — and the defining property of this class is that the reported state is correct and the output is not. **Existing output monitoring, correctly inventoried:** `dags/monitor_memdb_batch_output.py` (GCS file count + threshold + lookback, on the TPA membership output — not a model output), plus `dags/monitoring/ipdsc_monitor.py` (five `GCSObjectExistenceSensor`s on `ipdsc/dt=/data_source_id={42,14,46,49,65}/_SUCCESS` plus a registry loop, `schedule='5 0 * * *'`), `dags/monitoring/advertiser_scores_monitor.py`, and `dags/monitoring/dag_run_duration_watchdog.py`. **Monitoring exists but sits one level downstream of the loss** — on the daily `ds_49` output, not the hourly `site_network_hourly` partitions — and `dag_run_duration_watchdog`'s `WATCHDOG_RULES` covers only `dag_id='audience_intent'`. 50 of 122 model files write `dt=` partitions; none has an existence check `[measured]`.

**The rate is a bound in a specific direction.** 33/2,217 = 1.49% is measured over three models **selected because they carry the swallowing loop** — selection on the dependent variable. Broadening to all 23 `feature_group_1_source` datasets and all 20 `ipdsc` data_source_ids over the same 30 days surfaced no further silent partition loss (the two exceptions are a correct vendor skip and a model whose output legitimately starts 2026-09-02). So **1.49% is a rate for the swallowing-loop population and a lower bound fleetwide.** Absence is detectable from a listing; a partition that *was* written from partial input is not, and 50 `optional=True` call sites mean that mode is widespread and uncounted.

**Not Poisson.** 11 of the 17 absent `site_network_hourly` hours fall in 2026-08-14..08-16 and are plausibly one infrastructure event. Do not project 2.44% as a daily hazard.
