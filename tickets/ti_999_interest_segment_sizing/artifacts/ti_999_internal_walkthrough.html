Part 1 — Foundations

Audience Family Definitions

Before we discuss permutations we need a precise taxonomy. MNTN's bidder distinguishes four targeting families. Each has a different set of data sources, different scoring, and a different intended use.

List-based Retargeting (formerly called "1P" in earlier passes)

What it is
Customer LISTS the advertiser uploaded (CRM list, IP list, hashed emails resolved to IPs). Buyer-selectable; the advertiser chooses to include or exclude.
Data sources
DS4 (CRM), DS8 (IP List), DS47 (CRM Identity Graph Generated)
Scored by MNTN
No. The bidder targets these IPs because they're on the advertiser's list, not because they've been scored for intent.
Intended use
Retargeting — re-engaging the advertiser's known customer base, or excluding existing customers from prospecting.
Naming correction (per user 2026-05-28): earlier passes called this "1P" (first-party). The accurate term is "List-based Retargeting" — the underlying data IS first-party, but functionally it's a retargeting product. Distinguishes from "MNTN First Party" (pixel data) — see next slide.
Operational note: DS4 in a POSITIVE clause = retargeting campaign. DS4 in a NEGATIVE clause inside a prospecting expression = customer suppression (still prospecting). Adjacent family — auto-attached, not buyer-selectable

MNTN First Party (pixel data) — NOT in this analysis

What it is
MNTN's pixel fires on the advertiser's website. Captures site-visit and conversion events. Becomes "MNTN First Party data" the bidder uses for retargeting and exclusion.
Data sources
DS21 (MNTN Conversion), DS34 (MNTN Pageview)
Buyer-selectable?
No. Pixel data is auto-attached. Buyers typically see it appear in NEGATIVE clauses in prospecting expressions: "exclude past visitors / converters." It's not a positive-targeting choice.
Why we don't analyze it here
Because it's not buyer-selectable, it's not part of the "which segments should the buyer pick?" question that drives TI-956. We keep DS21/34 in scope as exclusion clauses (where they naturally appear) but don't bucket on them.

What it is

Part 2 — Permutations

The Permutation Space

What a campaign actually targets is determined by three orthogonal axes. We need to walk through each axis before we can interpret the data.

9 / 56

Three orthogonal axes determine a campaign's behavior

Axis 1: Presence

Which families are referenced at all: any combination of {MM, 1P, 3P}. 2³ = 8 base permutations.

Axis 2: Polarity

For each present family, is the clause positive (include) or negative (exclude)? A family can appear with both polarities in a single campaign.

Axis 3: Structure

For positive clauses, structure determines whether they UNION (OR-additive) or INTERSECT (AND-restrictive) with other positive clauses. The expression JSON's op:or vs op:and nodes encode this.
Together they define what the campaign actually targets. We'll walk through each axis with empirical data, then look at every meaningful permutation.

Axis 2: Polarity — positive vs negative clauses

For each family present in a campaign, the clause can be positive (an op:any outside a not subtree) or negative (inside a not).

NotationMeaningSet semantics
+MMMM family in a positive clauseIP must match MM segment(s)
-3P3P family in a negative clauseIP must NOT match the listed 3P segment(s)

+MM, +3P, -3PMM positive AND 3P positive AND a separate 3P negativeMixed polarity on same family — also occurs in practice

Empirical observation

  • MM is essentially never used in negative clauses (we have it in <10 campaigns).
  • 1P is overwhelmingly negative (96% of 1P clauses are exclusions).
  • 3P is overwhelmingly positive (98% of 3P clauses are inclusions).
  • Buyers use these families with very specific intents — not arbitrary combinations.

Axis 3: Structure — OR-additive vs AND-intersect for positive clauses

The audience expression is a tree of operators. Positive op:any leaves can be combined two ways depending on the structure of their parent / sibling operators.

StructureBuyer-writtenSet semanticsBidder behavior
OR (additive)OR(MM, 3P)(in MM) ∪ (in 3P)EXPANDS reach — adds 3P IPs not in MM
AND (intersect)AND(MM, 3P)(in MM) ∩ (in 3P)NARROWS MM — only IPs in both
AND NOT (exclude)AND(MM, NOT 3P)(in MM) ∖ (in 3P)NARROWS MM by removing 3P matches

Why this matters

The earlier verbal model said "MM combines targeting clauses with AND-intersection." This is empirically wrong for inclusion clauses. We'll see in Part 4 that 92% of MM-mixed inclusion expressions use OR(MM, 3P), not AND(MM, 3P). The bidder is honoring exactly what the buyer wrote — but the verbal model misunderstood what buyers write.

The bidder's mental model — empirically supported

  1. Eligibility = expression match. Per-bid-request evaluation. SSP sends bid request → bidder checks if IP matches the campaign's audience expression → bid eligible.
  2. Within eligibility, household_score shapes preference + CPM. Bidder prefers MM-scored IPs while they remain available in the bid stream + pacing windows.
  3. MM ceiling exists per (campaign × MM segment × day) — saturation point where MM-scored IPs in the bid stream are exhausted relative to pacing budget.
  4. When ceiling hits, bidder falls through to unscored eligible IPs — most relevant for OR-additive 3P clauses that brought in non-MM-scored IPs.
This refines two earlier model claims:
  • The "MM combines targeting clauses with AND-intersection" verbal model was WRONG for inclusion clauses — they're OR-additive, and the bidder honors that.
  • The "bidder strictly prefers scored, falls through to unscored" model is RIGHT — empirically validated by FICO's cross-bucket ceiling test (Part 5).

Part 5 — MM ceiling

The MM Ceiling — Why OR-include Overflow Happens

OR-include only matters if the bidder actually reaches the 3P-added IPs. That requires MM scoring to "exhaust" relative to pacing — the MM ceiling.

31 / 56

What "MM ceiling" means

  • For each (campaign × MM segment × day), there's a finite supply of MM-scored IPs the bidder can actually bid on.
  • This supply is bounded by: MM segment size, the share of those IPs that appear in the bid stream, frequency caps, pacing logic.
  • If the campaign's daily budget exceeds what MM can absorb at quality, the bidder must either pause or bid on non-MM eligible IPs.
  • For campaigns with OR(MM, 3P) expressions, the non-MM eligible IPs are the 3P-added IPs — and the bidder bids them. Result: unscored delivery share rises.
The MM ceiling is what makes OR-additive 3P inclusion meaningful. Without ceiling overflow, the 3P clause is a paid-for filter the bidder never reaches.

Appendix

Methodology

  • Window: 30d ending 2026-05-28. Active campaign = ≥1 impression in window.
  • AST parser: JS UDF in queries/ti_999_clause_polarity_ast.sql. Tracks op:not ancestor depth and op:or group ID per leaf. Distinguishes OR-additive vs AND-intersect vs AND-NOT structures.
  • Bucket classification: based on presence/absence of MM (DS13/38/46), 1P (DS4/8/47), 3P (DS17/18/35) in positive/negative clauses + OR/AND structure.
  • Spend metric: media_spend + data_spend + platform_spend from silver.summarydata.sum_by_campaign_by_day.
  • Conversion metric: click_conversions + view_conversions.
  • Visit metric: HLL_COUNT.MERGE(site_visitors) (distinct visitor sketch).
  • Delivery distribution: regex extraction of household_score, advertiser_household_score, realtime_conquest_score from cost_impression_log.model_params for 2026-05-26.
  • Per-segment CVR: equal-attribution proxy (campaign metrics ÷ N picked dscids). Matches Alex Knorr's TI-956 default target_weight='equal'.
  • MM ceiling test: FICO single-advertiser cross-bucket comparison (campaigns 525934 and 325113) + bucket-aggregate scored-imps-per-$K.
54 / 56

Caveats

  • Descriptive, not causal. Bucket-level KPI differences may reflect advertiser-mix / vertical / funnel-stage selection effects. Controlled tests (A/B by clause structure within same advertiser) would tighten claims.
  • Single-day score-distribution snapshot (2026-05-26). Multi-day pattern stability not verified. Direction is robust; magnitudes are noise-bounded for small cohorts (e.g., 5b MM AND NOT 3P with 27K imps).
  • Equal-attribution per-segment CVR divides campaign metrics by dscid count. Over-credits dscids in narrow campaigns; under-credits in wide campaigns. True per-segment causal isolation requires Alex's TI-956 Performance axis (LOO-baseline).
  • Quintile rank stability over time not verified. Some Q1 dscids may move to Q4 next week. Multi-week ranking needed before policy decisions.
  • MM ceiling is per-(campaign, MM segment, day). The FICO test shows it at advertiser scale but specific per-segment ceiling values require per-MM-segment delivery scan.
  • Inferred IP overlap. Direct overlap measurement via federated household_scoring__v1 wasn't feasible (scan timed out). Used inference from delivery distributions instead.
