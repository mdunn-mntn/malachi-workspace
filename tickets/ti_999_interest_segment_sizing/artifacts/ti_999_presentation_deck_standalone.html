Why Buyers Pick Interest Segments Blindly
…and what TI-956 does about it

~$43M/yr of MNTN spend is allocated to the worst-performing LiveRamp segments at the same rate as the best — because buyers have no quality signal.

Malachi Dunn · Targeting Infrastructure · 2026-05-28 · TI-999 Finding 15 · 30d window · 15,529 active campaigns · $40.42M

The case in five steps

1. MNTN's bidder distinguishes 4 targeting families. Only one — MM (Mountain Match) — produces per-IP quality scores. The rest are unscored.

2. Buyers compose campaigns from clauses across these families. The same families combine in different ways via OR (expand), AND (narrow), or AND NOT (exclude) — and the bidder behaves differently in each.

3. When buyers add OR-include 3P clauses to MM campaigns, the bidder bids unscored 3P-added IPs once MM ceiling exhausts. This only happens in 17.7% of MM+3P campaigns. The other 76% pay for 3P clauses that aren't being reached.

4. Where 3P inclusion DOES drive delivery, the segment a buyer picks matters enormously: top-quintile LiveRamp segments convert at 350x the rate of bottom-quintile. Buyers currently spend equally on each tier (no quality signal).

5. Per-segment quality scoring (TI-956) → conservative 50% substitution = ~1.6M incremental conversions/year.

§1 — Audience family definitions

We can't talk about permutations until we precisely define the families.
MNTN's bidder distinguishes four — each with different data sources, different scoring, and different intent.

List Retargeting (shown as "1P" in tables for brevity)

What it is
The advertiser uploads a CUSTOMER LIST (CRM, IP list, hashed emails resolved to IPs). The bidder targets those IPs because they're on the list.
Data sources
DS4 (CRM) · DS8 (IP List) · DS47 (CRM Identity Graph Generated)
Scored by MNTN?
No.
Functional type
Retargeting — not prospecting. Positive clauses re-engage known customers; negative clauses suppress known customers from prospecting.
Important taxonomy note: earlier passes called this "1P" (first-party). That's overloaded — MNTN ALSO has a "MNTN First Party" product (pixel data, DS21/34) that isn't buyer-selectable and isn't in this analysis. To avoid confusion: this slide is what we mean by "1P" in subsequent table headers. It's List Retargeting.

3P — Bought interest segments

What it is
Behavioral / interest segments BOUGHT from external data providers. The provider says "these IPs are interested in X" and MNTN uses it for audience matching.
Data sources (active set)
DS17 (ShareThis) · DS18 (Dstillery) · DS35 (LiveRamp IP)
Scored by MNTN?
No. There is no per-segment quality signal in the UI today. A buyer searching "$100K+ income" returns ~90 LiveRamp results with no way to differentiate them.
Catalog scale
~213k active LiveRamp categories — 97% of active 3P by count. LiveRamp = 99.6% fresh; ShareThis + Dstillery = 100% >2-year stale at metadata level.
Functional type
Prospecting via described interests.

MM — Mountain Match (the MNTN-scored family)

What it is
MNTN-derived audience targeting — IPs scored by MNTN's own models using verticals, keywords, behavioral signals, and recent-site conquesting.
Data sources
DS13 (Vertical Categorization) · DS19 (RTC — Real-Time Conquesting) · DS38 (BUK / UI Audience Keywords) · DS46 (ML Audience Intent — Fangorn)
Scored by MNTN?
YES. Produces two scoring signals at impression time:
• household_score — graduated 0-10000 (from DS13/38/46)
• realtime_conquest_score — binary 10000/-1 qualifier (from DS19)
Empirical scoring distribution (5/26, 61M imps)
household_score: 65% = -1 (unscored), 15% = 10000 (top), 11% = 8k-10k (HI), ~10% middle.
realtime_conquest_score: 95% = -1, 5% = 10000.
Functional type
Prospecting + conquesting via MNTN's quality models. The only family where the bidder has per-IP MNTN quality information.

Axis 3: Structure — OR-additive vs AND-intersect vs AND-NOT

What buyer writesSet semanticsEffect on MM scoring
OR(MM, 3P) — union(in MM) ∪ (in 3P)EXPANDS reach — 3P brings IPs that aren't in MM's scored set (unscored)
AND(MM, 3P) — intersect(in MM) ∩ (in 3P)NARROWS MM — only IPs in both. Stays in MM's scored universe.
AND(MM, NOT 3P) — exclude(in MM) ∖ (in 3P)NARROWS MM — removes 3P-matched IPs. Stays scored.
The earlier verbal model said "MM combines targeting clauses with AND-intersection." That's wrong for inclusion. Buyers overwhelmingly use OR (union/expand), not AND (intersect/narrow). The bidder honors what buyers write — but the verbal model misunderstood what buyers write.

Logic of each major pattern — what the buyer is asking the bidder to do

PatternWhat buyer intendsWhat the bidder does
MM only"Bid on MM-scored IPs"Bids MM scored IPs (4% baseline unscored noise)

1P + 3P (no MM)"Bid lists + interests"Bid 1P ∪ 3P with no MM ranking — anti-pattern
The two patterns to focus on: "MM OR 3P" ($2.15M / 30d, dominant 3P pattern — where 3P brings unscored IPs) and "3P only" ($5.24M / 30d, no MM scoring at all).

§3 — What each permutation actually delivers

Counts and spend tell us what BUYERS write.
Score-distribution at delivery time tells us what the BIDDER does.

Three structurally distinct delivery patterns — empirically

% impressions on IPs with household_score = -1 (unscored). 2026-05-26 delivery, ~61M imps. MM OR 3P brings 3.3x more unscored delivery than MM_only baseline; MM AND 3P narrows-but-stays-scored; MM AND NOT 3P is the cleanest scored delivery of any cohort.

Why OR-include brings unscored: the MM ceiling

  • For each (campaign × MM segment × day), there's a finite supply of MM-scored IPs the bidder can actually bid on (bounded by segment size, bid stream availability, pacing, frequency caps).
  • If campaign budget exceeds what MM can absorb at quality, the bidder must either pause or bid on non-MM eligible IPs.
  • For OR(MM, 3P) campaigns, "non-MM eligible IPs" = the 3P-added IPs the buyer's expression brought in.
  • This is OK behavior — the buyer wrote OR, the bidder honors it. The question is WHAT 3P segments the buyer picked.
The MM ceiling is what makes OR-include meaningful. Without ceiling overflow, the 3P clause is a paid-for filter the bidder never reaches.

FICO case study — same advertiser, same MM vertical, two campaigns

MM_only at $41.7K → 71.5K scored imps/day. MM+3P OR at $168.5K (4x spend) → essentially the same 60.1K scored + 236K unscored 3P-added impressions/day. MM ceiling is real and binds at scale.

FICO 325113 — the actual buyer-written expression

{ "categories": { "where": {
  "op": "and", "value": [
    { "op": "or", "value": [                          // ← top-level OR (additive)
      { "op": "any", "value": {
          "data_source_id": 13,                       // MM Vertical
          "category_ids": [111001] }},
      { "op": "any", "value": {
          "data_source_id": 35,                       // LiveRamp 3P (9 dscids)
          "category_ids": [/* 9 */] }}
    ]},
    { "op": "any", "value": {                          // AND: must ALSO be in 17 more LiveRamp dscids
        "data_source_id": 35,
        "category_ids": [/* 17 more */] }},
    { "op": "not", "value": { "op": "or", "value": [/* past visitors */] }}
  ]
}}}

Buyer explicitly wrote OR. The bidder honors it: eligible IPs = (in MM 111001) ∪ (in any of 9 LiveRamp) ∩ (in 17 more LiveRamp) AND NOT (recent visitor). Result: 79.7% unscored delivery — the 17-LiveRamp constraint narrows eligibility to LiveRamp-resolved IPs, most of which aren't in MM's scored set.

§4 — When does OR-include actually overflow MM ceiling?

OR-include only matters if the bidder actually reaches the 3P-added IPs.
If MM never exhausts, the 3P clause is dead weight.
How often does MM actually exhaust?

Only 17.7% of MM+3P campaigns are actually ceiling-bound

76.3% of MM+3P_incl_only campaigns sit below MM ceiling. Their 3P clauses are paid for but the bidder isn't reaching them. $1.6M/30d (~$19M/yr) of dead-weight 3P spend.

The fix — semantics buyers should actually use

What buyer writesEffectWhen this is correct
OR(MM, 3P)Expands MM with 3P-added unscored IPsOnly when MM is actually ceiling-bound (~18% of MM campaigns). Otherwise the 3P clause does nothing.
AND(MM, NOT bad-3P)Narrows MM to exclude low-quality 3P segments — makes MM more specific and nicheAlways usable — buyers can refine MM by excluding noisy 3P. But buyers DON'T use this pattern today (only 7 campaigns).
AND(MM, NOT 1P)Suppresses past customers from MM prospectingAlready widely used (296 camps, $1.88M / 30d). The standard hygiene pattern.
The under-used pattern: AND-NOT-3P (filter out junk 3P from MM). Mechanically clean — produces 99% scored delivery. But buyers don't do it, because the UI doesn't surface "here's a bad 3P segment you should exclude." TI-956 unlocks this pattern by ranking segments — low-ranked become candidates for exclusion.
