## Opener — Contrast :00–:30 (62 words)

> *[Power Pause. Three beats of silence on slide 1. Lower pitch, measured pace.]*
>
> *[Advance to slide 2.]*
>
> "Every media buyer in America believes the same thing.
>
> *[Two beats.]*
>
> CTV is a new channel. CTV is a risky channel. CTV is *not* paid search.
>
> *[Advance to slide 3. Pause.]*
>
> **They're wrong. All three.**
>
> CTV *is* paid search. Same intent. Richer context. Bigger screen. And every dollar buyers hold back from CTV is a dollar they're paying more for — for less information.
>
> Let me show you."

**Why it lands:** Contrast opener. The three-beat objection → "They're wrong. All three." is the first punch. Declarative, no hedging. This is the line execs will repeat.

## Act 1 — Disruption :30–1:30 (133 words)

> *[Advance to slide 4.]*
>
> "Here's the conversation Alex and I keep having.
>
> A sophisticated marketer. A CMO. Someone who's spent a decade mastering Google. They tell us the same thing every time.
>
> *[Slow down. Let the quote on screen land.]*
>
> *'We trust paid search. We trust paid social. CTV is the unknown.'*
>
> For years we tried to argue them out of it. Reach. Brand lift. Attribution. We ran the playbook. It never worked.
>
> *[Pause. Three beats.]*
>
> Because they weren't wrong to be scared. They were being asked to move money out of a system they understood, into a system they didn't.
>
> *[Advance to slide 5.]*
>
> Here's what we finally figured out. The whole conversation was built on **a lie**.
>
> CTV is not a different channel than paid search. CTV is the *same* channel. Buyers just can't see the wiring."

**Delivery:** Conversational on the setup, then drop pitch hard on "a lie." That's the turn.

## Act 2 — Revelation 1:30–3:00 (198 words)

> *[Advance to slide 6.]*
>
> "So let's show them the wiring.
>
> CTV works when? When somebody visits the advertiser's website after the ad served. That's the outcome. That's what buyers pay for.
>
> One question.
>
> *[Pause. Hold on the slide 6 question.]*
>
> How does that person get to the website?
>
> *[Advance to slide 7. Deliver flat.]*
>
> They **search**. They type the brand into Google. They click. They land.
>
> *[Advance to slide 8.]*
>
> Every CTV conversion runs on search intent. We are not stealing budget from Google. We're riding the exact same signal Google rides on — delivered on a screen Google cannot touch.
>
> *[Pause. Three beats.]*
>
> And then we go further.
>
> *[Advance to slide 9.]*
>
> Paid search sees two words: *'athletic shoes.'* That's all Google gets. That's all Google can sell.
>
> BUK — our Bottoms-Up Keywords — scrapes the advertiser's website and captures what users actually do once they arrive. Every click. Every page. Every product.
>
> **Paid search sees the query. We see the behavior.**
>
> Same dollar. Richer signal. That is not competition. That is an upgrade."

**Delivery note:** Slides 6 → 7 is the hinge of the whole Loom. Sell the question first. Let it hang. Then flip to the answer.

## Act 3 — Resolution 3:30–4:30 (148 words)

> *[Advance to slide 11.]*
>
> "Three things. Every buyer. Every exec. Every one of us.
>
> *[Count them — build pace, each takeaway a beat faster.]*
>
> **One.** CTV is not a different channel. It is paid search, running on the same intent Google runs on, delivered on a screen Google cannot reach.
>
> **Two.** BUK enriches that intent in a way paid search *literally cannot*. Google sees the query. We see what the user actually wants.
>
> **Three.** Moving budget from search to CTV is not a risk. It is not a trade. It is an *upgrade*. Same dollar. Richer signal. Television reach. The math is not close.
>
> *[Pause. Reset pace to slow.]*
>
> *[Advance to slide 12.]*
>
> So we've been having the wrong conversation.
>
> The question is not *'why CTV instead of search?'*
>
> The question is — **why would any buyer pay for the thin version of a signal when we can give them the thick one?**"

## What changed from v1 (sharpening for impact)

- **Opener:** Replaced two-part contrast with three-beat objection → "They're wrong. All three." Hardest possible flip.
- **Act 1:** Swapped "false premise" for "**a lie**." Same idea, sharper edge. Cut the "we'd talk reach, brand lift, attribution" line down to three fragments.
- **Act 2:** Restructured around the repeated parallel — "Paid search sees the query. We see the behavior." Every sentence drops a word from v1.
- **Story:** Cut from 78 words to 56. Short fragments. "Past the black. Past the white. Stops on the pink." Rhythm does the work.
- **Act 3:** Kept the Rule of Three but tightened each line. Replaced "the math isn't close" with "The math is not close" — period, not contraction.
- **Close:** Added the declarative sentence before the Power Line ("That's the line") so the final words land with gravity, not explanation.

Net: 732 words → 668 words. More white space for pauses, which is where impact actually lives.

## Delivery Cheat Sheet

- **Pace:** 150 wpm baseline. Slow to ~120 on the Revelation (Act 2) and the Power Line. Speed to ~170 on the Rule of Three.
- **Pauses:** Three beats before the Power Line (every time). Two beats after each "one / two / three." Zero rushing.
- **Pitch:** Drop pitch on every bolded phrase. Especially "a lie," "literally cannot," and the Power Line.
- **Eye contact:** Into the lens. For the Power Line, hold the lens without blinking for the duration of the sentence.
- **Alex co-present option:** Hand him slides 6–9 (the wiring + the compare). He's the technical voice — let him own "how the engine works." You open (slides 1–5), you deliver the story (slide 10), you close (slides 11–13).
