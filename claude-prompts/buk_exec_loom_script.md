# BUK Exec Loom — Final Script (~5 min)

**Audience:** Paulo Black (VP Eng), Richard Girges (CTO), Kale (Director, TI)
**Co-recording:** Alex Knorr
**Target runtime:** 5:00 | **Word budget:** ~750 (150 wpm)
**Actual word count:** 732

---

## Power Line (final)

> **"CTV is paid search — upgraded."**

Five words. Exec-repeatable. Bookends the script.

**Alternate 1:** "CTV isn't replacing search. It's search done better."
**Alternate 2:** "Same intent. Richer context. Bigger screen."

---

## Opener — Contrast :00–:30 (68 words)

> *[Power Pause. Three beats of silence looking into camera. Then begin — lower pitch, measured pace.]*
>
> "Here's what every media buyer on the planet believes: CTV is a **new channel**. A **riskier** channel. A **different bet** than paid search.
>
> *[Pause. Two beats.]*
>
> And here's what the data actually says — CTV isn't a different bet. It's **the same bet**. Same intent. Richer context. Bigger screen.
>
> Every buyer still protecting search budget from CTV is paying more for a thinner version of the same signal. Let me show you."

**Screen cue:** Black slide. White text: *"CTV isn't a new channel."* On the word "same bet," slide transitions to: *"It's paid search, upgraded."*

**Why this opener:** Contrast opener from the playbook. Names the buyer objection, flips it in the same breath. No throat-clearing, no "today I'm going to talk about."

---

## Act 1 — Disruption :30–1:30 (148 words)

> "Every week, Alex and I sit across from sophisticated marketers — CMOs, growth leads, people who've spent a decade mastering Google and Meta. And every week, we hear the same objection.
>
> *[Slow down. Emphasize the italicized phrases.]*
>
> *'We trust paid search. We trust paid social. CTV feels like the unknown.'*
>
> We used to argue with that. We'd talk reach. We'd talk brand lift. We'd talk attribution. None of it moved them.
>
> *[Pause.]*
>
> Because they weren't wrong about what they were afraid of. They were afraid of making a channel bet on something they didn't understand. Shifting a dollar from Google to CTV felt like shifting from a known system to a black box.
>
> Here's the problem with that whole conversation. **It's built on a false premise.** CTV is not a different channel than paid search. CTV is running on the *same engine*. Buyers just can't see the wiring."

**Screen cue:** Simple two-column slide — left: *"What buyers believe: CTV ≠ Search."* Right: *"What's true: CTV = Search, enriched."* (Build in the right column on the word "false premise.")

**Speaker notes:** Use the word "we" deliberately throughout — this is MNTN's collective challenge. Unity principle.

---

## Act 2 — Revelation 1:30–3:00 (217 words)

> "So let's pop the hood.
>
> *[Slow. Deliberate. This is the core of the Loom.]*
>
> When a CTV ad works, how do we know it worked? The **site visit**. Somebody sees the ad, then they show up at the advertiser's website. That's the outcome. That's the currency.
>
> Now ask the next question — the one nobody's asking. **How does the user get to the website?**
>
> *[Pause. Let them answer it in their head.]*
>
> Almost always, they search. They type the brand into Google. They click a result. They land.
>
> Which means every CTV conversion is already riding on search intent. **We are not competing with Google. We are riding on the same signal Google rides on** — we just show up on a screen paid search can't touch.
>
> *[Pause. Beat of three.]*
>
> And here's where it gets interesting.
>
> Paid search only sees the **query** — 'athletic shoes,' 'running shoes,' 'CRM software.' That's it. That's all Google can sell them.
>
> We go further. Our Bottoms-Up Keywords work — BUK — scrapes the advertiser's website and maps what users *actually do* once they're on the site. Not the query that got them there. The intent revealed by their behavior. Every click. Every page. Every product.
>
> That's not the same signal paid search has. That's a **richer** version of it."

**Screen cue:** Animated reveal.
- Frame 1: "CTV works → site visit."
- Frame 2 (builds): "Site visit ← search."
- Frame 3 (builds): "CTV rides on search intent."
- Frame 4 (hero): "Paid search sees the query. BUK sees the behavior."

**Speaker notes:** This is the revelation. Speak slower here than anywhere else. Let the logic chain stack in their heads one step at a time.

## Story — Nike Beat 3:00–3:30 (78 words)

> "Take Nike. A runner in Denver — tired of looking like every other runner on the trail — types 'athletic shoes' into Google.
>
> *[Pause, drop pitch.]*
>
> Google sees those two words. She lands on Nike dot com, scrolls past the black sneakers, clicks the **bright pink trail runners**, adds them to cart.
>
> Paid search never sees that moment. **We do.**
>
> To Google, she's an 'athletic shoes' shopper. To us, she's a 'colorful trail running shoe' shopper.
>
> Same dollar. Sharper aim."

**Hall framework check:**
- **Character:** runner in Denver
- **Emotion:** tired of looking like every other runner
- **Moment:** scrolls past the black sneakers, clicks the pink trail runners
- **Specific detail:** bright pink trail runners, adds to cart

**Screen cue:** Side-by-side. Left: Google's view ("athletic shoes"). Right: our view ("colorful trail running shoes"). Let the asymmetry do the work.

---

## Act 3 — Resolution 3:30–4:30 (150 words)

> "So three things I want every buyer, every exec, and every one of us to walk away with.
>
> *[Count them on fingers. Build pace — each takeaway a beat faster.]*
>
> **One.** CTV is not a different channel. It's the same intent signal paid search runs on — delivered on a screen paid search can't reach.
>
> **Two.** BUK enriches that signal in a way paid search *literally cannot*. Google sees the query. We see the query plus everything the user did next.
>
> **Three.** Shifting budget from search to CTV is not a trade-off. It is not a risk. It is an **upgrade**. Same intent dollar. Richer context. Television reach. The math isn't close.
>
> *[Pause. Reset to slower pace.]*
>
> Which means the conversation we've been having with buyers — *'why CTV instead of search?'* — is the wrong conversation. The right conversation is: *'why would you keep paying for the thinner version of this signal when we can give you the thicker one?'*"

**Screen cue:** Three-takeaway slide. One line each, large type, direct-labeled. No bullets — three horizontal bars with a single phrase in each.

**Speaker notes:** Rule of Three. Keep each takeaway short and declarative. No hedging words ("may," "might," "could"). This is the resolution — own it.

---

## Close 4:30–5:00 (71 words)

> "The story we tell buyers needs to change.
>
> CTV isn't a new channel asking for their trust. It's paid search — the thing they **already** trust — upgraded.
>
> *[Pause. Slow way down.]*
>
> Same intent. Richer context. Bigger screen.
>
> That's the line. That's what we want buyers walking into their next budget meeting believing.
>
> *[Final Power Line — deliver flat, confident, no upward inflection.]*
>
> **CTV is paid search — upgraded.**
>
> Our job is to make sure every room they're in knows it."

**Screen cue:** Return to opening slide — *"CTV is paid search — upgraded."* Let it hold for three seconds after the final word. Bookend close.

**Speaker notes:** No "any questions." No "thanks for watching." End on the Power Line, let it breathe, then fade to black. Two heartbeats of silence before the Loom ends.

## Delivery Cheat Sheet

- **Pace:** 150 wpm baseline. Slow to ~120 wpm on the Revelation (Act 2) and the Power Line. Speed to ~170 wpm when stacking the Rule of Three in Act 3.
- **Pitch:** Drop pitch on every bolded phrase. Power Pauses before the Power Line — three full beats of silence.
- **Energy arc:** Low-measured (Opener) → deliberate (Act 1) → building (Act 2) → personal (Story) → confident (Act 3) → quiet-final (Close).
- **Eye contact:** For Loom, look into the camera lens, not the preview. Every sentence of the Power Line, hold the lens.
- **If Alex co-presents:** hand him Act 2 ("So let's pop the hood..." through the BUK reveal). He's the technical voice — let him own the "how the engine works" beat. You open, you close, you deliver the Power Line.
